# Songuess shared 3D assets

Open `songuess-assets.blend` in Blender. The **Shared assets** scene contains the
reusable jacket and record at their local origins. The **Reveal reference** scene
contains linked copies, a studio camera, lights, and a 52-frame reveal study at
30 fps: a full sleeve swivel followed by the record sliding into its mouth.
The saved file opens at the final pose. Scrub frames 1–52 to inspect it.
`RevealAssembly` parents both objects; their final local depths are zero, so
the record sits inside the jacket even when the assembly is tilted.
**Sleeve fit inspection** provides the same final insertion with an unrotated
assembly, making the space between panels easy to inspect from the side.
The **Spin reference** scene has a seamless clockwise 1.8-second loop (54
frames at 30 fps), with linear rotation and a Cycles modifier. The browser uses
the same speed during playback and follows playback after reveal without resetting
the angle. Once the reveal finishes, pausing or ending playback stops the spin. **Carousel reference** demonstrates the shared sleeves folding back
and the next cover being lifted, pulled forward, turned toward the viewer,
then gently settled for inspection (frames 1–31 at 30 fps). Each switch takes
about one second; the pull starts after the previous sleeve has returned.
Both shelf rows face the same way: left edge forward, right edge back.
Shelf positions have separate depth lanes, and oriented jacket volumes keep
the moving covers clear of their neighbors. The broad sheen stays fixed relative to the lighting while subtle
13 tapered sleeve rubs in three asymmetric clusters rotate around the play
control. Their widths and contrasting materials keep the motion readable
at the game's desktop and mobile display sizes. The browser briefly ramps up
the 33⅓ RPM spin and coasts when playback pauses.

Run `just blender-check` to verify the saved scene and physical record clearance
through every frame of insertion, plus carousel jacket clearance at every
frame and eighth-frame. Reload the updated `.blend` in Blender; an
already-open file retains its previous scene data until reloaded.

The sleeve has separate front/back panels, folded cardboard edges, a spine, and
an open right edge with separate paper lips. Its 0.052-unit inner gap fits the
0.028-unit record; the reveal keeps the spinning disc between both panels. `SleeveArtwork` is a separate UV-mapped front surface, so the
browser can replace its texture with any album cover. The vinyl is an annular
mesh with a real spindle hole, fine incised grooves between flat playing bands,
and a larger red paper label with a faint pressed center ring and printed
SONGUESS, 33 1/3 RPM, SIDE A, and STEREO lettering. The lettering is mesh
geometry and rotates with the pressing. Its original
256 × 256 `vinyl-sheen.png` packs polymer variation in RGB and roughness in alpha;
its sheen UVs counteract the record rotation so the reflections remain fixed
while physical wear rotates. The Spin reference uses a matching material driver. Flat groove faces avoid
the raised ribbing produced by smoothing across the recessed channels. Materials use Principled BSDF and export as glTF PBR.
The placeholder artwork is original and generated by the script.

## Rebuild

From the repository root, with Blender installed:

```sh
just blender-build
just blender-check
```

This runs `build_assets.py` in Blender background mode with audio disabled and
recreates the compressed `.blend`, placeholder, sheen texture, both preview renders, manifest, and
the two runtime exports under `frontend/public/models/songuess/`. No Python
packages, downloaded textures, Draco decoder, or external Blender add-ons are
required. This was built with Blender 5.2.2 LTS.

## Runtime contract

| Export | Root | Size in model units | Replaceable surface |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `sleeve.glb` | `Sleeve` | 2 × 2, about 0.076 deep | `SleeveArtwork` mesh/material |
| `vinyl.glb` | `Vinyl` | radius 0.94, about 0.028 deep | `VinylLabel` mesh/material |

Exports use glTF coordinates: +X right, +Y up, +Z toward the viewer. Model origins
are centered. Rotate the vinyl around local Z. Artwork fills the front square;
textures applied with Three.js must use `flipY = false` and sRGB color space.
The models export without cameras, lights, or animation clips. The reference
animation is editable in Blender; the website implements its motion in TS so it
can respond to game state, reduced motion preferences, and viewport size.
Rapid navigation accelerates the current gesture and combines pending input
into the latest requested position, avoiding repeated mid-gesture restarts.

The Blender source is about 680 KB. The exports total about 1.54 MB.
`previews/vinyl.png` provides a standalone front view of the revised pressing. These are ordinary small Git files; Git LFS is not
needed. `asset-manifest.json` records current export sizes and triangle counts.
Blender backup files (`.blend1`, `.blend2`) are ignored.
